Yes! You can use *=swaynag=* or more commonly *=rofi=* itself to show available bindings.

The most popular approach is *=sway-which-key=* or displaying a menu with =rofi=/=wofi= when entering a mode.

*** Simple approach: show a notification with =notify-send=

When entering the mode, also fire a notification listing the keys:

#+begin_src nix
"${modifier}+m" = ''exec notify-send "Kevin mode" "c=calibre g=chromium e=emacs s=signal w=wifi r=refresh j=emoji l=lock v=vlc o=exit"; mode kevin'';
#+end_src

*** Better approach: =swaynag= as a hint bar

#+begin_src nix
"${modifier}+m" = ''exec swaynag -m "c=calibre | g=chromium | e=emacs | s=signal | w=wifi | r=refresh | j=emoji | l=lock | v=vlc | o=exit" -t warning; mode kevin'';
#+end_src

But you'd need to kill swaynag on =mode default=.

*** Best approach: =i3-mode-bindings= / =which-key= style

There's *=sway-which-key=* (a community tool):
- https://github.com/MaxVerevkin/sway-which-key

In nixos, check if it's in nixpkgs:
#+begin_src bash
nix search nixpkgs sway-which-key
#+end_src

It reads your sway config and automatically displays current mode bindings in a popup — exactly like Emacs which-key.

Then update the mode-entry keybindings to also launch =wlr-which-key=:

#+begin_src diff
-          "${modifier}+Shift+m" = "mode monitor";
-          "${modifier}+m" = "mode kevin";
-          "${modifier}+r" = "mode resize";
+          "${modifier}+Shift+m" = "mode monitor; exec ${pkgs.wlr-which-key}/bin/wlr-which-key ${wkMonitor}";
+          "${modifier}+m" = "mode kevin; exec ${pkgs.wlr-which-key}/bin/wlr-which-key ${wkKevin}";
+          "${modifier}+r" = "mode resize; exec ${pkgs.wlr-which-key}/bin/wlr-which-key ${wkResize}";
#+end_src

> *Note:* The =wlr-which-key= process will stay open until a key is pressed or =Escape=. Since sway handles the actual keypress and then does =mode default=, the popup will linger — you may want to also kill it on mode exit, or rely on the fact that =wlr-which-key= auto-closes on any keypress if it's configured that way. Check your version's behavior.
